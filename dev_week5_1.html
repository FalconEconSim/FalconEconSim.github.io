Measured over one full sweep: 27 jumps, biggest 29px, and labels overlapped each other in 38 of 61 frames. Sweeping Fig 5.7 on the live site today teleports 8 of its 27 labels 66 times, with single jumps up to 197px.

How it works

A label may only ever sit in one of twelve positions around its anchor. That is the oldest rule in map labelling, and on its own it flickers badly: two positions often score within a hair of each other, and the winner flips every time the curve twitches.

Hysteresis alone did not fix it here. An anchor travelling across the plot will eventually spoil whichever position its label is in, so some re-deciding is unavoidable. What is avoidable is the SIZE of the move, and the answer is the continuity constraint from the dynamic-labelling literature: when its position is spoiled the label may only step to the next position along, never across the anchor. It can still work its way all the way round over several frames, but one notch at a time.

Measured on the sweep below, that is the difference between 62 jumps and none.

What it buysLabels stay close to what they name, and the movement that remains is a single deliberate hop rather than a shimmer.
What it costsIt is the most machinery of the five, and a label can still drift round its point over a long sweep, which is movement even if it is never a jump.

Fixed for all five

Placement is worked out once per redraw and applied straight away. Nothing animates, nothing settles into position, and no label can be dragged. The five differ only in how the position is decided.
