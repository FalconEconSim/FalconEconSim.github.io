Week 5 leader testbed

Five ways to make the leader arrive

Placement is settled: approach 1, twelve slots and one notch of movement at a time. What is left is the dashed line, which sometimes stops in white space. These are five ways of fixing that, on a deliberately harder figure.

Why it misses

The leader is drawn from the anchor the label was given. Measured over 41 frames on the current page, 72 of 328 leaders ended more than 6px from anything drawn, and the failures are not spread around: the curve labels anchor to a real sampled point on their curve and land at 0px, while the two straight-line labels anchor to a hand-picked coordinate that was never on the line and miss by up to 21px in 28 of 41 frames.

So the line is not wrong, the target is. All five attempts below start from the same change: a label names an object rather than a coordinate, and the point of contact is computed from that object every frame. They differ in what they do with it.

One thing the harder figure exposed

Sixteen labels instead of eight is enough to strain the placement itself. On this figure two labels end up overlapping in 25 frames of 61, which never happened on the simpler one. That is the placement, not the leader: all five attempts overlap identically.

Letting a boxed-in label step out one ring clears it completely, 0 frames of 61, but it costs steadiness: moves over 25px go from 12 to 46 and the biggest from 49px to 92px. Since you said the placement works as it is, I have left it alone rather than trade that away inside a page about leaders. It is a separate call, and the numbers are here when you want to make it.

1. Snap the anchor onto the object

The anchor a figure declares is moved onto the thing it names before anything is drawn.

2. Shortest link

The leader goes to whichever point of the object is closest to where the label actually ended up.

3. Contact dot

The shortest link, plus a small dot marking exactly where it lands.

4. Elbow leader

Straight out of the side of the label, then a turn and a straight run to the object.

5. Touch, do not point

Labels sit close enough to read as attached, and only the ones pushed away get a leader at all.
