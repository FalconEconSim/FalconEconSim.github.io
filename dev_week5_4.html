Press Sweep both and watch the labels rather than the curves. The meter counts a jump only when a label moves much further than its anchor did, so a label travelling with the point it names is not held against it.

Measured over one full sweep: 0 jumps, biggest 0px, and labels overlapped each other in 0 of 61, but a label is hidden in 32 of 61 frames. Sweeping Fig 5.7 on the live site today teleports 8 of its 27 labels 66 times, with single jumps up to 197px.

How it works

The position is worked out on the first frame and then never worked out again. The label is welded to its anchor and moves exactly as the anchor moves, so there is no per-frame decision left that could flip.

When two labels do end up on top of each other the lower-priority one is hidden instead of being moved. That is the trade the temporal-labelling literature recommends: dropping a label is less distracting than making it jump.

What it buysAbsolutely steady, and the label keeps a constant relationship to its point, which makes it feel physically attached.
What it costsThe offset chosen on the first frame may be a poor one later, and the hiding is not rare: over the sweep below something is hidden in 32 of 61 frames, and at its worst only 4 of the 8 labels are on screen. A label that vanishes is its own kind of distraction.

Fixed for all five

Placement is worked out once per redraw and applied straight away. Nothing animates, nothing settles into position, and no label can be dragged. The five differ only in how the position is decided.
