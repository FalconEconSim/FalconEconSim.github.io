Studioweeks 2, 4, 8

The same course pages, with three things added: a new typeface, equations whose numbers you can drag, and a Python notebook for each week that runs in the browser.

1

Allo, a friendlier type

Allo is an open-source face by Josh Puckett, redrawn from Inter. It has a roundness axis, so the studio sets reading text a little soft and headings and numbers rounder:

Q* = 4sharp Q* = 4friendly Q* = 4round

Numbers that change while you drag use tabular figures, so they don't jitter.

2

Live equations

Borrowed from Bret Victor's Tangle and the marimo widget that sent us here: the plum numbers in a formula or a sentence are handles. Drag one sideways and every result, sentence and chart recomputes; while you drag, the chart keeps a ghost of where you started.

They sit next to the existing figures as the algebra behind the picture, not instead of them.

3

A notebook per week

Real Python (numpy and matplotlib) running in your browser with Pyodide: nothing to install, nothing sent anywhere. Edit a cell, press Shift+Enter, and every cell below re-runs. Knob cells put draggable numbers on top of the code.

Each notebook downloads as a Jupyter file or opens in Google Colab.

The three weeks

02

Consumer Preferences & Choice

Live equations for the tangency test (is this bundle the best?) and for Cobb-Douglas demand. The notebook finds the best bundle by brute force and checks it against the formula.

FiguresNotebook
04

Demand Elasticity

Elasticity at any point on a linear demand curve, and the revenue rectangle split into its price and quantity effects. The notebook computes elasticity along the whole curve and hunts for the revenue-maximizing price.

FiguresNotebook
08

Welfare & Monopoly

The monopoly two-step rule with every number live, and the welfare it costs. The notebook maximizes profit numerically and tests a claim about surplus shares on thousands of random markets at once.

FiguresNotebook
