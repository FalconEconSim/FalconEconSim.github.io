Section 1 · Deriving Demand
Fig 3.1Two-Panel Demand Derivation

The top panel is the consumer's choice problem (X-Y space); the bottom panel is the demand curve (PX-X space), drawn directly below so the X axes line up. Lower PX and the optimum in the top panel slides right; the dot in the bottom panel slides down the demand curve. The dashed line ties the two together at the same X*.

Consumer choice (indifference curve & budget)
Resulting demand curve
Price of X: $6.0
That is the demand curve: each price you set produces one optimal X*, and the locus of (PX, X*) pairs is the demand curve. Demand is derived, not assumed.
Section 2 · Decomposing a Price Change
Fig 3.2Slutsky Decomposition

When PX rises, total demand falls from A to C. Slutsky splits that into two steps. Substitution effect (A→B): rotate the new, steeper budget line until it can just afford the original bundle A — the move along the curve from relative-price change alone. Income effect (B→C): remove that compensation and the consumer slides down to their actual new budget. Note: this is the Slutsky compensation (afford old bundle), not the Hicks compensation (hold utility constant).

New (higher) Price of X: $10.0
Why it matters: the substitution effect is always negative (price up → quantity down along the curve). The income effect's sign depends on whether the good is normal or inferior.
Fig 3.3Income Effects & Demand Shifts

Hold prices fixed and change income M. In the top relationship the optimum bundle moves out along the income-expansion path; in the demand panel the whole demand curve shifts rather than moving along it. That is the difference between a shifter (income) and a price change.

Income M: $120
Shift, not movement: a higher income shifts demand right at every price. Only a change in the good's own price moves you along a fixed demand curve.
Section 3 · Practice
Fig 3.4Shift vs. Movement Drill

For each event, decide: does the demand curve for coffee shift, or do we just move along it? Own-price changes are movements; everything else is a shift. The deck reshuffles forever.

Fig 3.5Demand Function Explorer

The Cobb-Douglas demand function is X* = αM/PX. Change PX to move along the curve; change income M or the preference weight α to shift / rotate the whole curve. The faint ghost is the baseline.

Read price PX: $6.0
Income M: $120
