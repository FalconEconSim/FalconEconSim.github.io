Fig 2.4, ten layout approaches

Same chart, same numbers, same controls in all ten. Only the way the figure spends its width changes. Open a few side by side and tell me which one to take to the rest of the site.

Fixed in all ten, because these had one right answer rather than ten

  • The arrows no longer cross the tick numbers. The board reserves a gutter and splits it into two lanes: numbers next to the axis, movement arrows beyond them.
  • Neither curve label sits on its curve. Each is anchored at a different point along its own line and pushed off it at a right angle, away from the other line, with a leader back to the anchor.
  • The verdict is live. The old "quick test" was static text that said the slope had changed while the readout beside it said it had not.
  • "No change yet" now reads "Unchanged".
  1. 01Ratio splitReplace the fixed 360px readout column with a ratio. The chart keeps about 64% of the figure at every width instead of surrendering whatever the readout does not use.
  2. 02Chart over readout stripChart takes the full width of the figure; the readout becomes a wrapping strip underneath it.
  3. 03Aspect-ratio switchThe split is chosen by the shape of the window, not its width. A landscape laptop gets the chart and readout side by side; a portrait or square window stacks them.
  4. 04Readout overlaid on the chartThe chart fills the whole figure. The readout floats as a compact card in the plot area that the budget line always leaves empty (the upper right).
  5. 05Full bleedThe figure breaks out of the reading column entirely and runs to the window, with the readout in a narrow rail. Prose keeps its measure; the diagram does not have to.
  6. 06Container queryThe figure responds to the width of its own container rather than the viewport, so it lays itself out correctly whether it sits in a prose column, a sticky bay, or full bleed.
  7. 07Height drivenThe chart is sized from the height of the window rather than the width of the column, so a wide short laptop screen spends its vertical space on the diagram instead of leaving it blank.
  8. 08Progressive disclosureThree numbers that answer the question stay visible; the full table of intercepts and slopes sits behind a disclosure. The chart gets the width by default and the detail is one click away.
  9. 09BENCH bayThe redesign treatment: the chart and its controls pin to the viewport in a bay on the right while the explanation scrolls past on the left, so the sentence and the thing it describes are on screen together.
  10. 10Fixed aspect frameThe chart lives in a frame of a fixed shape that scales to fill whatever width is available, with a hard legibility floor. If the readout would push the frame below that floor, the readout wraps underneath instead of squeezing the chart.
