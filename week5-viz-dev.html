Fig 5.1 · Production

Total, Marginal & Average Product

Hold capital fixed and add labour one worker at a time. The total product (TP) curve rises, bends over, and eventually peaks. Directly below (on the same labour axis) sit the marginal (MP) and average product (AP) curves. Drag the labour slider: the dashed projection line ties the point on TP to the matching MP and AP values beneath it, so the three-stage relationship is physical, not mental.

Fig 5.2 · Production

Isoquant & the MRTS

An isoquant shows every (L, K) combination that produces the same output Q. Its slope is the marginal rate of technical substitution, the rate at which capital can be traded for labour while holding output fixed: MRTS = MPL/MPK. Raise output to push the isoquant outward (the dashed ghost marks where it was); slide the labour position to read the MRTS as it flattens along the curve.

Fig 5.3 · Production

Returns to Scale

Returns to scale ask what happens to output when you scale all inputs by the same factor. With Cobb-Douglas Q = LaKb, the sum a + b decides it: a + b > 1 is increasing, = 1 is constant, < 1 is decreasing returns. Toggle the regime and watch the Q = 100 / 200 / 300 isoquants morph: the spacing between equal-output steps is the tell. Transitions ease over ~400 ms rather than snapping.

Fig 5.4 · Production

Isoquant Shape Drill

The shape of an isoquant encodes the substitutability of inputs. Smooth and convex ⇒ Cobb-Douglas (imperfect substitutes). A straight line ⇒ perfect substitutes (constant MRTS). An L-shape ⇒ perfect complements / fixed proportions (Leontief). Read each scenario and pick the technology; you'll get contrastive feedback on every miss.
