Fig 7.1 Profit Landscape (TR vs TC)

The firm earns profit wherever TR > TC. Drag the price slider to change the slope of the TR line (revenue from each unit sold). The vertical gap between TR and TC is shaded green when positive and red when negative. The bottom panel shows the profit curve directly. Drag the vertical cursor to see MR vs MC at any quantity, and find q* where the slopes are equal (MR = MC).

Total Revenue (TR) vs Total Cost (TC)
Profit π(q) = TR − TC
Market Price P*: $10.00
Key insight: The firm maximizes profit where MR = MC (the revenue from one more unit exactly equals its cost). To the left of q*, MR > MC (produce more). To the right, MC > MR (cut back).
Fig 7.2 Profit Rectangle Anatomy

The profit rectangle has height = P* − SAC(q*) and width = q*. The area = total profit. Use the price slider to change P*. Toggle "Show Common Mistakes" to see ghost rectangles using SAVC or SMC as the bottom edge (both wrong).

Market Price P*: $7.00
Fig 7.3 Shutdown Decision Zone Map

Use the price slider to set P*. The colored zone shows whether the firm should shut down, operate at a loss, break even, or earn positive profit. Click "Next Step" to walk through the algebraic proof of the shutdown rule.

Algebraic Proof: When to Produce
Produce if operating loss < shutdown loss:
Step 1: Profits > −TFC  →  keep producing
Step 2: TR − TVC − TFC > −TFC
Step 3: TR − TVC > 0  →  TR > TVC
Step 4: P·q > AVC·q
Conclusion: P > AVC  ✓
Market Price P*: $6.00
Fig 7.4 Shutdown Manager Simulation

You are the firm manager. Review the scenario, then decide: keep operating or shut down? After choosing, see the correct answer and the full economic reasoning.

Fig 7.5 Supply Curve Emergence

Watch the price sweep upward. Below the shutdown price (min SAVC = $4), the firm supplies nothing. Above it, the SMC curve lights up and each supply point traces out in the right panel, building the supply curve in real time.

Firm: SMC and SAVC
Supply Curve (builds as P rises)
Press "Animate" to watch the supply curve emerge.
Fig 7.6 MR Decomposition

Toggle between a price-taking firm (horizontal demand, MR = P always) and a firm with market power (downward-sloping demand, MR < P). In market power mode, click "+ 1 unit" to see the gain (new unit revenue) and loss (price cut on existing units) that make up MR.

Price P*: $8.00
Quantity q: 3
Fig 7.7 Three-Panel Demand Shock

The market mechanism has three linked panels: the typical firm (left), the market (center), and the typical consumer (right). Choose a shock scenario and click through the steps to see how causality flows from consumer to market to firm.

Choose a scenario and click through the steps to trace the causal chain.
Fig 7.8 Market Supply Builder

Market supply is the horizontal sum of all individual firm supply curves. Drag the number-of-firms slider to see how market supply expands and the equilibrium price falls as more firms enter the industry.

Individual Firm Supply
Market Supply (horizontal sum)
Number of firms n: 10
Key insight: At each price, add quantities across all firms; never add prices. The market supply curve is steeper than any individual firm's supply curve but shifts right as n grows.
Fig 7.9 Long-Run Entry/Exit Machine

Start with 50 firms earning positive profit (P* = $8, green rectangle visible). Click "Run Entry" to watch firms enter, supply shift right, price fall, and the profit rectangle shrink to zero at long-run equilibrium (n = 100, P* = $7). Click "Run Exit" to go the other direction from a loss situation.

Market (Supply and Demand)
Firm (Profit Rectangle)
Zero economic profit is healthy: it means the firm earns exactly its opportunity cost. Owners are compensated fairly, just not earning excess returns.
