Week 5 label testbed

Five ways to place a label

Same figure, same content, same slider, five different rules for deciding where a label goes. All five place the label at once and none of them can be dragged: they differ only in how steady the label looks while the slider moves.

The problem they are answering

Placement is currently worked out fresh on every redraw, and each solve is independent of the last. When two candidate positions score almost the same, a small change in the curves flips the winner and the label crosses the plot. Sweeping one slider through 24 steps on Fig 5.7 teleports 8 of its 27 labels, 66 times in total, with single jumps up to 197px.

The research calls this temporal coherence, and it is a known failure of applying a static placement algorithm frame by frame. Each page below carries a live meter so you can see for yourself how often a label jumps.

Chosen: 1, sticky slots

With one fault left: the dashed leader sometimes stops in white space rather than reaching the thing it names. Five attempts at fixing that, on a deliberately harder figure.

1. Sticky slots

Twelve fixed places around the anchor, and it may only ever step to the next one along.

2. Labels at the end of the line

The name sits at the end of the line it names, the way most newsrooms label a line chart.

3. Boundary rail

Every label lives in a fixed column beside the plot, joined to its anchor by a leader.

4. Welded offset

The offset is chosen once and then welded to the anchor. Clashes hide a label rather than move it.

5. Lanes

Each label owns a horizontal band. It slides left and right with its anchor but never changes row.
