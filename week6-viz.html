§ 6.1

Cost Minimization Sandbox

The firm wants to make a given output as cheaply as possible. In Fig 6.1 below, slide the point along the isoquant to your best guess for the cost-minimizing bundle (L, K). Pull the dashed isocost line in or out to test cost levels, and point at any bundle to read what it costs. The reveal shows the tangency and the step-by-step solution; afterwards change the wage w and the rental rate v and watch the cheapest bundle move.

§ 6.2

Two-Panel Cost Derivation

Top panel: the isoquant map, where the firm finds the cheapest bundle for each output. Bottom panel: the total cost curve TC(q). In Fig 6.2 below, find the cheapest bundle for one output and what it costs; the reveal then steps through the output levels and maps each cost-minimizing bundle to a TC value, building the curve point by point.

Cobb-Douglas production: each input is imperfectly substitutable. The expansion path is a ray through the origin, meaning the firm scales both inputs proportionally as output rises. The TC curve is linear in q (constant returns to scale here).
§ 6.5

SR vs LR Cost Curves

Short-run Marginal Cost (SMC), Average Variable Cost (AVC), Average Total Cost (ATC), and Average Fixed Cost (AFC) are drawn simultaneously. All four short-run cost curves in one diagram. In Fig 6.5 below, total fixed cost (TFC) changes: predict where the lowest point of ATC goes, then see how fixed costs shift ATC but leave SMC and AVC unchanged. The long-run average cost (LRAC) comes next, in Fig 6.6.

§ 6.6

LRAC Envelope Builder

Each SRAC curve corresponds to a different fixed level of capital (K = 1, 2, 3, 4, ...). The LRAC is the lower envelope of all SRAC curves: at each output level, the long-run optimum is whichever fixed-capital option is cheapest. Watch the animation below, then draw the envelope yourself in Fig 6.6.

After the reveal, add more plant sizes and see the LRAC approach a smooth curve.

§ 6.8

Minimum Wage Automation Simulator

Imagine you manage a firm. Currently, the government's minimum wage is exactly what you're paying your workers. Now the government passes a law raising the minimum wage. What do you think happens?

Think about it: if labor suddenly costs more, what would a cost-conscious manager do? Would you use the same amount of labor? Or would you try to use less labor and substitute toward capital?

Make your prediction in Fig 6.8 below: find the new cost-minimizing bundle and what it costs. Then watch the isocost line rotate and shift out, and see where the new cost-minimizing bundle lands. Does it match your intuition?

§ 6.10

Efficient Scale Finder

Fig 6.10 below plots an average cost curve. Find q* (the efficient scale), the output where AC is lowest. The reveal overlays the MC curve, which crosses AC exactly at the minimum, and shades economies of scale in green and diseconomies in red. Afterwards try the other average cost functions.
