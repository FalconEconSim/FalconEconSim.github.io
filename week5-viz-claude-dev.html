Production: Inputs, Output & Returns to Scale

Prof. Naveen Sunder

The firm's technology turns inputs into output. We start with one variable input (the total- and marginal-product curves and the law of diminishing marginal product), move to two inputs (isoquants, the MRTS, and returns to scale), and end at the Cobb-Douglas lab. Each figure is sized for its own job, tall stacks where panels share an axis, square frames where slopes must read true.

Section 1 · One variable input
Fig P.1Total & Marginal Product

A single tall canvas stacks two panels that share the worker axis. The top plots total product TP(L); its slope at the chosen L is the marginal product MPL, drawn as a tangent. The bottom plots that slope directly. The dashed vertical line ties the two readings to the same L, so "MPL = slope of TP" is literally one line.

Production function: iCobb-Douglas and the S-curve both show diminishing MPL. The linear form has constant MPL (no diminishing returns), and the quadratic eventually turns MPL negative.
Workers L: 3.0
Capital K (fixed): 4
Law of diminishing marginal product: with capital fixed, each extra worker eventually adds less than the one before. The TP curve flattens and the MPL curve slides down.
Section 2 · Two inputs: isoquants & the MRTS
Fig P.2Isoquant & Indifference-Curve Shapes

Three technologies, three square panels (square so slopes read true): Cobb-Douglas bows toward the origin, perfect substitutes are straight, perfect complements (Leontief) are right-angled. Toggle the interpretation: the same math is a firm's isoquant (axes L, K) or a consumer's indifference curve (axes X, Y).

Cobb-Douglas
Perfect substitutes
Perfect complements
Shape = substitutability: a convex isoquant means inputs substitute imperfectly; a straight line means they substitute one-for-one; an L means they must be used in fixed proportions.
Fig P.3MRTS Live Calculator

Slide the point along the isoquant Q = K0.5L0.5 = 4. The tangent redraws and the marginal rate of technical substitution MRTS = MPL/MPK = K/L updates: how many machines one extra worker frees up. A square frame keeps the slope honest.

Isoquant Q = 4, with tangent
Labor L: 4.0
Diminishing MRTS: move right (more L, less K) and each worker replaces fewer and fewer machines. That is exactly what makes the isoquant convex.
Section 3 · Returns to scale
Fig P.4Diminishing MP vs. Returns to Scale

Two different ideas, side by side. Left: hold K fixed, add L, MPL falls (a per-input story). Right: scale K and L together by the same factor, with a + b > 1 output more than doubles (a whole-firm story). One function, Q = KaLb, can do both at once.

a (capital): 0.60
b (labor): 0.60
MPL with K fixed = 4
Scale both inputs by λ
Fig P.5Returns-to-Scale Isoquant Spacer

A ray from the origin crosses the isoquants Q = 1, 2, 4. Under constant returns the crossings are evenly spaced; under increasing returns they bunch toward the origin; under decreasing returns they spread out. Set the exponents and watch the spacing move.

a: 0.50
b: 0.50
Fig P.6Input Substitution vs. Technological Change

Two very different moves. Substituting inputs slides the bundle along the existing isoquant , same technology, different mix. Improving technology shifts the whole isoquant inward , the same output now needs fewer inputs. The faint ghost marks where you started.

Section 4 · Synthesis & practice
Fig P.7Consumer ↔ Producer Analogy

The consumer and the firm solve the same problem with different names. Left: indifference curve, MRS, budget line, tangency. Right: isoquant, MRTS, isocost line, tangency. Move the slider and the reference curve shifts on both panels at once.

Utility / output level: 2.6
Consumer: IC + budget
Firm: isoquant + isocost
Same engine: "tangency of a convex curve to a straight budget/cost line" is the optimum in both worlds. MRS = price ratio for the consumer; MRTS = input-price ratio for the firm.
Fig P.8Cobb-Douglas Production Lab

The full multi-variable explorer for Q = A·KaLb. Set the parameters and an evaluation point (K, L); read off the isoquant map, the returns-to-scale verdict (a + b), MPL, MPK, and the MRTS. The λ-scaled bundle tests the returns-to-scale definition directly.

Fig P.9Bakery Hiring Simulator

A bakery with fixed ovens (K). Hire workers one at a time and watch total product climb, the marginal worker's output shrink, and the wage bill grow. Keep hiring and MPL eventually goes negative. The simulator refuses the hire. Buy an oven to lift the whole curve.

Wage w: $15
Fig P.10Isoquant-Shape Quiz

A production function appears: predict its isoquant shape, then reveal it. Smooth-convex (Cobb-Douglas), straight (perfect substitutes), or L-shaped (Leontief / fixed proportions). The deck reshuffles forever.
