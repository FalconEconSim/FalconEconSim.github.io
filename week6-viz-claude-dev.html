Costs: From the Cheapest Bundle to the Cost Curves

Prof. Naveen Sunder

Production told us what's possible; cost theory asks what's cheapest. We start where the isocost line is tangent to the isoquant (cost minimization), trace that optimum across output levels to build the total-cost curve, then unpack the short-run family (MC, AVC, ATC, AFC) and the long-run average-cost envelope. Square frames for the input diagrams, wide frames for the cost curves.

Section 2 · The short-run cost family
Fig C.3Short-Run Cost Curves

Marginal cost (MC), average variable cost (AVC), average total cost (ATC), and average fixed cost (AFC) on one wide canvas. MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimums; the gap between ATC and AVC is exactly AFC. Raise the fixed cost and watch ATC lift while MC and AVC stay put.

Total fixed cost: 40
iMC pulls the averages: when MC is below an average, that average falls; when MC is above, it rises. So MC must cross AVC and ATC exactly at their lowest points.
Fixed costs only move ATC: AFC = TFC/q shrinks as output grows, so ATC and AVC squeeze together at high q. MC and AVC are built from variable cost alone, so the TFC slider never touches them.
Fig C.4The LRAC Envelope Builder

Each plant size has its own short-run average-cost (SRAC) curve. The long-run average cost is their lower envelope — at every output, pick the plant that makes it cheapest. Add more plant sizes and the scalloped envelope smooths toward the LRAC.

Number of plant sizes: 4
Section 3 · Applications
Fig C.5Minimum-Wage Automation

You manage a firm producing a fixed output q. Raise the minimum wage and the isocost line rotates steeper — the cost-minimizing bundle slides up the isoquant toward more capital, less labor. The faint ghost is where you started. Substitution toward automation, drawn live.
