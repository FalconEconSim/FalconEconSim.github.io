Section 1 · The Externality Wedge
Fig 11.1

Negative Externality & the Pigouvian Tax

Demand is \(P = 100 - Q\) and private marginal cost is \(PMC = 10 + Q\). Each unit also dumps a marginal external cost \(MEC\) on third parties, so social marginal cost is \(SMC = PMC + MEC\). The market produces where \(PMC = MB\) (too much); the optimum is where \(SMC = MB\). The red triangle is the deadweight loss. Apply the Pigouvian tax \(t^{*} = MEC\) to lift private cost onto the social-cost curve and erase it.

Marginal external cost MEC: $20
Section 2 · Least-Cost Abatement
Fig 11.2

Cap-and-Trade: Equalising Marginal Abatement Cost

Two firms must together abate a total amount of pollution. Firm A cleans up cheaply (\(MAC_A = 2e_A\)); Firm B is expensive (\(MAC_B = 8e_B\)). A permit market makes every firm abate up to the same permit price, so the low-cost firm does more of the work. The result meets the target at least cost, far below forcing both firms to abate equally. Slide the total cap to see the permit price and the saving move.

Total abatement required: 100
